## Throughput and the "ready for prime time" claim

a16z frames infrastructure as "(almost) ready for prime time" (p.32). Aggregate blockchain throughput rose from **<25 TPS in 2020 to ~3,400 TPS in 2025 (100x+ growth)**, against Stripe's Black Friday peak (~2,300), Nasdaq open-hours (~2,400), and credit cards (~24,500) (p.33). The report also surveys privacy (Zcash, Railgun; OFAC lifting Tornado Cash sanctions in 2025) (p.38) and quantum risk (**6,650,280 BTC / $746B** at risk; NIST transition by 2035) (p.40).

## Ethereum L1 + L2 scaling (the key fee-compression chart)

The central Ethereum-ecosystem chart (p.35) is titled "Recent Ethereum upgrades shifted activity to L2 blockchains, driving record throughput and all-time-low fees." Headline numbers: it cost **$24 to transact on Ethereum L1 in November 2021** (peak) versus **<1 cent to transact on Ethereum L2s in September 2025** (p.35). Combined **Ethereum + Rollups throughput rose from 13 TPS (July 2020) to 236 TPS (September 2025)** (p.35). The chart tracks **Arbitrum, Optimism, and Base** L2 transaction costs individually — all collapsing toward ~$0 after Dencun/blobs — while Ethereum L1 remains the higher, spikier line (p.35). This is the report's explicit post-Dencun blob-impact view and the empirical basis other documents in this corpus cite for "L2 fees under a cent."

## Builder and developer interest by chain

Founder/builder interest, share of founders (p.10): **Ethereum ~23%, Base ~17%, Solana ~16.5%, Bitcoin ~5%, Polygon ~4.7%, Arbitrum ~4.5%, Optimism ~2.7%, Hyperliquid ~2.5%, Avalanche ~2.2%, TON ~2%**. Monthly open-source developers by chain (p.10): **Ethereum ~9K, Solana ~4K, Bitcoin ~2.5K, Base ~1.5K, Polygon ~1.3K, Cosmos ~1.2K, Polkadot ~1K, Arbitrum ~1K, Sui ~0.8K, BNB Chain ~0.7K**. The report's gloss: "Crypto is multichain, with Ethereum (and its L2s), Solana, and Bitcoin attracting the most developers" (p.10) — note that Ethereum-family interest (L1 + Base + Arbitrum + Optimism) sums well above any single competitor.

## Stablecoin distribution by chain

Adjusted stablecoin volume (total ~$1.2T) settles most on **Ethereum and Tron**, then Unichain, BNB Chain, Solana, Base, Arbitrum, Polygon, Aptos, and Avalanche (p.21). The sub-cent, sub-second stablecoin transfers the report touts are attributed specifically to **Solana and Ethereum L2s** (p.19).

## RWA value by chain

The RWA-by-chain table (p.27, count / total value) is dominated by Ethereum: **Ethereum 431 / $9,655.0M**, ZKsync Era 41 / $2,454.3M, Polygon 272 / $1,139.2M, Arbitrum 110 / $919.3M, Avalanche 40 / $742.9M, Aptos 14 / $720.1M, Solana 83 / $673.2M, BNB Chain 3 / $519.7M, Stellar 20 / $504.5M, XRP Ledger 11 / $360.8M (p.27). Top RWA issuers: BlackRock ($2,640.3M), Anemoy ($1,135.2M), Paxos ($1,134.0M), Tether Holdings ($944.0M), Franklin Templeton ($717.5M) (p.27).

## Bridge flows and network-revenue share by chain

Net 2025 bridge flows (p.36) show **Ethereum** with the largest net inflow (~$50B+), followed by Arbitrum, Hyperliquid, Solana, and Base. Bridge volume by project (p.36): **Hyperliquid canonical $73.9B**, LayerZero $50.8B, Circle CCTP $38.9B, Stargate $16.1B, deBridge $12.1B. The network-revenue chart (p.37) is the report's most pointed chain claim: Bitcoin and Ethereum dominated fee/tip revenue historically, but **"Solana and Hyperliquid are capturing the majority of real economic value today — a big shift from Bitcoin and Ethereum's dominance in years past"** (p.37). So: Ethereum still leads on stablecoins, RWAs, developers, and bridge inflows, but no longer on fee revenue.

## Solana specifics

Solana metrics (p.34): **~1,300 non-vote TPS**, **average transaction fee $0.00433**, and peak app revenue **~$620M** led by memecoins and trading tools, then DEX, wallet, MEV, lending, and launchpad categories.

## AI × crypto (brief)

The report's AI section (p.41-45) argues crypto may be "the only viable payment rail for AI agents" (Gartner: $30T in agent-influenced purchases by 2030), notes AI×crypto rose to **~26% of crypto VC deals** by 2024-25 (p.43), and flags AI concentration (88% of "AI native" revenue = OpenAI + Anthropic; 94% of data-center GPU = NVIDIA) (p.45).
